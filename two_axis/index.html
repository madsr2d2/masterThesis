The two-axis block

Exps 135–151 · BnOH · 25 °C · pyrophosphate · 119 curves · pH 5.47–9.73

Seventeen runs, seven cuvettes each, one substrate and one buffer and one temperature: exps 135–151 are the only place in this archive where both concentration axes move inside a run, which is what the block is named for. What has not been written down is that they are also a pH ladder — exps 136–142 carry one set of seven compositions and exps 143–151 another, matched cuvette for cuvette — and that second design is the stronger one. This folder is what the block says when both are used.

1 · What the block is

All 119 cuvettes of all 17 runs, on the two concentration axes. Each run steps the substrate at its own top peroxide and the peroxide at its own top substrate, sharing one corner. The block moves both axes; no cuvette moves both at once, so an interaction between them is not identified anywhere in it.

Every run is an L: four cuvettes step the substrate at the run's top peroxide, four step the peroxide at the run's top substrate, and they share a corner. So the block moves both axes and no single cuvette moves both — an interaction term between them is not identified anywhere in it, which is the same shape of gap as the 0-of-88 crossing that leaves the buffer question open.

The concentration axes are within-run — 100.0% of the log[S] variance and 94.1% of the log[H₂O₂] variance sits inside experiments — so a per-run offset absorbs the day, the pH, the enzyme batch and the cell, and the orders are read from contrast between cuvettes of one run. The pH axis is the mirror image: only 11.5% of the log[HOO⁻] variance is within-run, so it needs the opposite control, and section 3 gives it one.

Two things stand out. The maximum rate has almost no substrate order — +0.091 ± 0.052 over a 50-fold ladder — while the initial rate has a real one at +0.374 ± 0.066. And the peroxide order is well below one: +0.794 ± 0.077, with strict first order rejected at F = 32 over 2.447–163.165 mM.

This is the one block where that substrate number means what it says. Everywhere else in the archive substrate was added by volume and displaced buffer, so [S] and [buf] move together at −0.96 in logs and a substrate order is an order in the pair; here [buf] is 75.013 mM on all 119 curves (induction.composition_collinearity).

3 · The pH axis, at matched composition

The strong runs of the two fixed-composition ladders. Each thin line joins one cuvette across the runs of its ladder — the same substrate, the same peroxide, the same enzyme, only pH differing — so the dashed slope is a pH order with the composition held in a per-cuvette offset. It is a half order, not a first order.

The block holds two composition sets — exps 136–142 and exps 143–151, sharing a substrate ladder and differing only in their peroxide levels — so inside either set a cuvette can be followed from run to run with only pH changing, and a per-cuvette offset holds its substrate, its peroxide and its position in the holder. The enzyme is what splits the ladders: it steps 0.014, 0.021, 0.034, 0.069 mM between runs and a cuvette offset cannot absorb it, so a ladder is runs sharing a composition and a loading.

4 · Is [HOO⁻] the reactant?

[HOO⁻] can be raised by adding peroxide or by raising pH, and the block moves both. If the chemistry consumed the hydroperoxide anion and nothing else, the two would give the same order. They do not — peroxide is the stronger lever in both statistics, so [H₂O₂] contributes beyond its own [HOO⁻] content, or pH costs something that opposes it.

The block has two independent levers on [HOO⁻] and they disagree by 3.4σ: raising it with peroxide at fixed pH gives +0.762 ± 0.046, raising it with pH at fixed peroxide gives +0.554 ± 0.040. A rate that were a function of [HOO⁻] alone could not do that.

Two readings survive, and the block cannot separate them. A second peroxide term — the buffer perhydrate of buffer §6 carries one, and [buf] is constant here so it would look like [H₂O₂] — or saturation, since the two contrasts sit at geometric-mean [HOO⁻] of 0.0329 and 0.0499 mM and the higher-level contrast is the one with the lower order, which is the direction saturation predicts. A 1.5× difference in level is a thin thing to hang 0.2 of an order on; a run crossing [buf] with [H₂O₂] would settle it, and the archive has none.

5 · What the curves do

Every live curve. Bars are each band's mean, and the count rises monotonically across all six. Peroxide alone is not enough: exps 136 and 137 sit at 73.4 mM with none at all, and they are the two weakest runs there — agreement 0.21 and 0.25 against 0.83–0.97. So the gas needs turnover, which makes it the catalysed decomposition.

Many of these curves rise, fall by more in one 60 s reading than the reaction moves in five, resume at the new level and do it again. Absorbance that goes away was never product — benzaldehyde does not un-form — so the falls are something leaving the light path. It is O₂ from the ketone-catalysed decomposition of the peroxide: the detachments ladder with [H₂O₂], they need turnover as well (exps 136 and 137 sit at 73.4 mM with none), and they are not synchronised between the cuvettes of a run — 17 coincidences over 357 cuvette pairs against the 16.0 independence predicts, which leaves the light path itself as the only place the absorbance can go.

The curve loses more absorbance in one 60 s reading than it gains in five, eight times over. Stitching the pieces together climbs toward the whole of the cuvette's substrate — on exp 135 cuvette 4 it passes it — because a bubble that costs δ when it leaves contributed δ of rise while it grew, and stitching keeps that rise. Subtracting the ramp conserves the net; the monotone bound assumes only that gas adds and product accumulates.

The repair that suggests itself is the one that must not be used. Stitched, exp 135 cuvette 4 ends at 1.26× the most absorbance its 0.219 mM of substrate could ever make, and against sawtooths planted into the block's own clean curves stitching recovers 1.15, 1.32 and 1.64 against 1.12, 1.24 and 1.58 — worse at every severity than leaving the curve alone. Subtracting the ramp each detachment reveals is unbiased while the artefact is the smaller half. Of 110 live curves, 13 sit above a load of 1 and carry no measurable rate; they are flagged, drawn here and in the counts, and not excluded. No order in this document moves under any repair.

Every live curve, by pH. The mark grows with the run's length, and the long runs sit at the bottom: the eight-hour runs are long because they are slow. What the acceleration tracks is pH — 27 of 42 above pH 9 against 23 of 68 below it.

The acceleration is a high-pH phenomenon, not a long-run one: 27 of 42 live curves above pH 9 accelerate past 3σ against 23 of 68 below it, and the eight-hour runs are the slow ones.

The 17 curves of the block whose rise finishes inside their run — the other 93 peak on the last reading and give only a lower bound. Below pH 9 the median is 1.41 turnovers and above it 3.31: where [HOO⁻] is scarce the catalyst goes round about once and stops, which is what a regeneration step that needs it would do. The sub-stoichiometric points are mostly in the runs concentration_agreement rejects.

Many of these curves rise to a maximum and then fall, and the quantity worth having there is how much product the catalyst made before it stopped, in units of the catalyst. curve_metrics.burst_amplitude reads it off the fitted curve — the two-phase solve trades amplitude between its exponentials without moving the curve, so B_fast is not the burst and neither is the sum. Only 17 of the 110 live curves finish their rise inside their run.

All 7 cuvettes of the block's one matched pair, both fits read at 3780 s — the largest window the two runs share. The runs differ in loading by 2.43× and in pH by 0.07 units, which §3's own pH order corrects out at 1.065×. The points sit on the first-order line, not the flat one: no dependence on catalyst is excluded at 3.6σ and first order is not, at 1.0σ.

Within runs the rise is nearly flat in substrate and strong in peroxide — +0.136 ± 0.045 and +0.793 ± 0.063 over the strong runs — and the catalyst cannot be asked that way at all, because [enz] never moves inside a run. The one matched pair the block holds says the rise is proportional to the catalyst: no dependence excluded at 3.6σ, first order admitted at 1.0σ. It rests on two runs, and the window sweep moves the apparent order between +0.77 and +1.35.

What the block does not support is an induction analysis. induction.signal_control regresses the landmark on signal-to-noise and returns +0.619 ± 0.228 here, so on this block the landmark is partly measuring the spectrophotometer and the 4OMe results do not transfer. The curve forms say the same thing from the other side: the block splits 56 two-phase against 54 one-phase and 46 lag-first against 64 burst-first, so an induction time averaged over it would average two different things.

At the other end of the curves, the block decelerates on a clock and not on its product — the elapsed-time coefficient is -0.697 ± 0.136 and the product coefficient +0.283 ± 0.129, which is the opposite of what the same test says about the 4OMe archive in product_fate.

6 · What the block cannot do

  • No enzyme-free curve. All 119 carry catalyst, and the pyrophosphate cell holds no blank at all, so nothing here can be staged against a background measured in its own buffer.
  • No interior point. The L identifies two orders and no interaction between them.
  • Enzyme is confounded with pH between the sub-series and constant within them, which is why section 3 measures inside a ladder.
  • No windowed statistic travels across it, at 9.6× in run length.
  • No mechanism fit has ever been run on it. data/fits/ holds one saved fit, on BnOH/25 °C/phosphate, and it shares no experiment with this block.
