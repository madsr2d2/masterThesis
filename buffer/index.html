What the buffer does, and to what

A reagent, a confound, and a candidate

The buffer is in this archive three times over — as a reagent, as a confound, and as the leading candidate for the step induction is trying to name — and had never been looked at as one question. ANALYSIS.md is the argument; this is the picture of it.

1 · A titration at one pH cannot name the species

At fixed pH the acid, the base and the total buffer are all proportional to one another, so an order in [buf] is an order in total buffer. That is what every order in this project is, including the three it inherits. The escape is to titrate at more than one pH — section 3.

2 · Five buffer titrations, not two

Every run in the archive that steps [buf] with [S] held fixed inside it — 4OMe-BnOH at 40 °C in phosphate, 82.5 mM H₂O₂, twenty curves. Only exps 32 and 34 have been used anywhere in this project before. At pH 7.00 the dependence is strong and saturates: +0.792 ± 0.185 over 3.125–25 mM and +0.371 ± 0.018 over 50–200. Above the pKa it is gone — −0.301 ± 0.459, +0.064 ± 0.082, +0.122 ± 0.039 — and the reason is the vertical gap, not the slopes: the buffer-independent rate is eight times higher there.

3 · Which species — and the archive cannot say

A buffer titration at one pH is an order in total buffer — at fixed pH the acid, the base and the total are proportional. Titrating at two pH values separates them: the base fraction runs 0.387 → 0.681 across phosphate's pKa, so general base predicts the catalytic coefficient rises 1.76× and general acid that it falls to 0.52×. The archive has that design and measures +1.06 ± 0.77 — which excludes nothing. The cause is figure B: above the pKa the buffer term is a small share of a much larger rate, so its coefficient is 44–127% uncertain there.

Exp 35 is dropped from the fit above because its own titration has R² 0.176 and is non-monotone, one cuvette at less than half its neighbours — a judgement about a run, made on the run's internal consistency and not on whether it helps. With it, the ratio is −0.08 ± 1.16 and excludes even less.

One titration at pH 6.5 would do what four runs at 7.0–7.5 cannot: the base fraction there is 0.17, so the two hypotheses separate by more, and the [HOO⁻] route that swamps the buffer term above the pKa is at its weakest.

4 · The buffer as a confound

Substrate was added by volume and displaced buffer, so [buf] falls 80 → 50 mM as [S] rises 1.85 → 7.4 in every 4OMe run. A substrate order measured there is an order in the pair, and two published ones have had to be corrected for it: the rate's (+0.45 → +0.58, which moved the level and not the trend) and the induction's (−0.121 ± 0.148 → −0.235 ± 0.158, which moved that route from excluding product control to not excluding it). Exps 135–151 are the one block where [S] moves alone — [buf] is constant across all seven cuvettes of all seventeen runs — and that is the block the in-scope fitting is scoped to.

The three buffers were each chosen for the pH they hold, which is what a buffer is for. The widest pH range any two share inside one substrate/channel cell is 2.01 units — Phosphate against Pyrophosphate on catalysed 4OMe — and those two cells share no peroxide: (82.5,) mM against (3.879, 195.882) mM. A range overlap is not a matched pair, and the check is on the values.

6 · Is the buffer a base, or is it carrying the peroxide?

If the catalyst is drawn into its active form by a species held in excess, that species' order on the rate and on the induction time must differ by exactly +1 — for every binding constant and every concentration, so it is a prediction with nothing to fit. The buffer axis meets it. The peroxide axis falls short on both blocks that carry a peroxide ladder, and both of those also fail the signal-to-noise control (hollow), as do the buffer's longest windows — which overshoot in exactly the direction that artefact predicts. Read only the filled points.

A buffer acting as a general base is a term in [buf]. A buffer that first takes up the peroxide itself — H₂O₂ + P ⇌ P–OOH, then P–OO⁻ delivering oxygen to the ketone the way a peracid or peroxymonosulfate does — is a term in [buf][H₂O₂]. At one pH the two schemes differ by that interaction and by nothing else, and this archive never varies it: of 88 runs, 53 step [buf] and 20 step [H₂O₂], and 0 step both. All five titrations sit at 82.5 mM peroxide.

What the archive does say points the same way twice. The pre-equilibrium constraint above is met on the buffer axis (+1.094 ± 0.150 against a required +1) and missed on the peroxide axis — so the species drawn into equilibrium with the catalyst behaves like the buffer and not like H₂O₂. And the buffer-free route rises with pH faster than one hydroperoxide can explain: 7.90× over 0.50 pH units at matched substrate, where [HOO⁻] gives only 3.23×. Both are eight-curve and two-run results across different days, and the archive's own between-day step is 1.80× — these are pointers, not measurements.

7 · What this settles, and what it does not

Settled. Every buffer order in this project is an order in total buffer. The catalysed turnover's dependence saturates — about first order below 25 mM, half order above 50 — and disappears above the pKa because the [HOO⁻] route grows past it, not because the buffer term shrinks. [S] and [buf] are collinear at −0.96 in every 4OMe run and at 0.00 in every in-scope run. Identity is confounded with pH everywhere.

Not settled: acid, base or spectator (+1.06 ± 0.77 against 1.76, 0.52 and 1.00), and whether the buffer is what carries E → E* — the induction's buffer order is -0.433 ± 0.201 on eight curves at 40 °C, where the induction is nearly over.

The two things that would settle both. A buffer titration at pH 6.5 and 25 °C on the catalysed 4OMe system — the low pH makes the buffer term the largest share of the rate it can be, the low temperature makes the induction thousands of seconds long, and one design gives the species and the induction's buffer order at once. And COMPUTATIONAL.md C7, whose model already carries a phosphate dianion beside the ketone hydrate for this reason.
