1 · Is the enzyme mismatch real?

Two of the six runs record [enz] 11.7% below the other four — 0.240683 against 0.272695 mM — and exp 16, the 40 °C run, sits between two runs at the higher value. A real restock is divided out; a transcription error is corrected. The two treatments differ, so this had to be settled before any fit.

From verify_enzyme_stock.audit(), which recomputes the cuvette concentration from the mass, molar mass, made-up volume and cuvette volumes recorded beside it — none of which the pipeline reads. Stocks change rarely and never change back, which is why the ringed point is the question: exp 16 is the only experiment in the campaign whose stock interrupts a run.

The weighing chain is self-consistent on 62 of 63 experiments and the compiled value equals the sheet's own kuv on 63 of 63. So both disputed values descend from a real weighing, 0.0203 g against 0.023 g, and the lower stock is used by nine experiments. A typed-over cell would break the chain; neither chain is broken. What the documents cannot settle is the ordering — exps 16, 17, 18 and 20 are byte-identical workbooks straddling the boundary. The kinetics settle it:

Each run's four rungs averaged. Rate divided by enzyme concentration should fall on one line against 1/T, so a concentration that is wrong for one run displaces that run away from the line the other five define. Forcing exp 16 to 0.273 doubles its distance, from −0.060 to −0.121, and moves it further out on all four rungs — the direction a too-large divisor gives.

Verdict: real. The recorded values win all four rungs on vmax and all four again on v0_quad, so the answer does not depend on the estimator. Every fit below divides by [enz] as recorded.

3 · What the composition is doing

The substrate ladder is not a substrate ladder alone, and the fix needed an experiment from outside this block.

Exps 32 and 34: 4OMe-BnOH at 40 °C and pH 7.00 with [S] held at 8.251 mM and only the buffer stepped. This is the design the BnOH set lacks entirely. Over 3.125–25 mM the order is +0.803 ± 0.173; over 50–200 mM it is +0.402 ± 0.024 (R² 0.993). The temperature series sits in the shaded band, so +0.40 is the value that corrects it.
H. Every run steps [buf] down 80 → 50 mM as [S] rises, identically. That is why the activation energies are unaffected — each rung is one fixed composition measured at six temperatures — and why the substrate order is not. I. With g = −0.325 and d = +0.402 the correction adds 0.13 at every temperature. The mean goes from +0.445 to +0.576 and the spread is 0.173 before and after: the correction moved the level, not the trend. Partial saturation in substrate, with no detectable temperature dependence.

3a · The fitting form: one relaxation, or two

The burst/lag form's rate is monotone, so it cannot hold a rate that rises to a maximum and then falls — and 11 of these 24 curves do exactly that. A second relaxation term gives it that freedom, and the two forms are exactly nested.

The forms are exactly nested — B₂ = 0 is the one-phase form — so the choice is an F test on two extra parameters (summary_kinetics.fit_progress). At 25, 35 and 40 °C the one-phase form sits at 1.5–3.0× noise and the second phase brings it to about 1.1×; at 15, 20 and 30 °C it is already at noise and nothing is selected. Selection is on F alone, deliberately not on τ₂ being resolved — τ₂ is pinned on only 2 of the 11, and requiring it rejected the clearest two-phase curve in the block (F = 791). Whether a phase exists and whether its time constant is determined are different questions.

4 · The activation parameters

The four rungs are four independent fits — the same substrate ladder recurs in all six runs — and they are offset because their composition differs, not because their slopes do. Individually they give 94.5, 86.0, 89.0 and 91.5 kJ/mol at ±2.6–3.0, which against their weighted mean is χ² = 4.68 on 3 dof, reduced 1.56: no composition dependence is detectable. Pooling by refitting one slope with a free intercept per rung gives 90.2 ± 1.5 kJ/mol.

Three routes now agree on ΔH‡. v_peak gives 86.3 ± 1.8 on all six temperatures; v_ss gives 86.6 ± 2.6 on the four where the one-phase form is sound and is not truncated; and vmax gives 87.6, which figure F measures as running 1.7 high from truncation — i.e. 85.9 corrected. Three estimators with unrelated failure modes, landing inside their errors, and the two-phase form reaches the answer without the estimator substitution.

vmax keeps the tighter error, ±1.5 against ±1.8, because a model-based peak inherits the fit's scatter and the τ grid is coarse. So: quote v_peak, and note that vmax agrees once its known bias is removed.

The induction is a different process. Its ΔG‡ is 12 kJ/mol lower, so it is the faster of the two, and its ΔS‡ is +4 rather than -58 — a much looser transition state. Its enthalpy, 93 ± 16, is not resolved apart from the turnover's 86 ± 1.8; the entropies are what distinguish them.

The fall has a name now, and it does not move these numbers

product_fate identifies the fall of section 3a as the oxidant attacking the aldehyde it has just made — the rate declines linearly in the accumulated product, A′ = v − kA, on 24 of 29 well-determined curves against 0 for the hyperbolic law product inhibition would give. So every rate in the table above is already net of that loss, and none of them is the production rate.

Both estimators, all 24 curves, one pooled slope each. v_prod is the production rate of the sink model with k pinned at slowdown.sink_activation's value; it sits 6.7% above v_peak on the median curve, and between -6.6% and +12.7% across the six temperatures with no order to it. So it shifts the level and not the slope: Ea moves by -2.96 ± 3.83 kJ/mol. The corrected line is the noisier one — Arrhenius scatter 0.189 against 0.099 — which is why it is reported and not applied. product_fate has the derivation.

The correction is reported and not applied, and the temperature series' own figures and parameters are unchanged by it.

Before comparing any of this to a calculation

  • Composite, not elementary. Every estimator here is a whole-turnover rate through a seven-step mechanism, so ΔH‡ is the barrier of whatever step is rate-limiting at this composition plus any pre-equilibria in front of it. Compare against the highest computed step, never a sum.
  • ΔS‡ assumes a pseudo-first-order rate constant, v/(ε·[enz]) in s⁻¹. The substrate order is +0.58, not 0, so the reaction is not saturated and that constant carries a substrate dependence. ΔH‡ does not depend on this; ΔS‡ and the absolute ΔG‡ do. Note what the intercept is quoted at: the median rung, [S] = 5.549 mM and [buf] = 60 mM.
  • First order in enzyme is assumed and untestable here — [enz] takes two values in this block, 11.7% apart. The archive's one test of it, a Selwyn test on exps 59 and 60, returns an order of +0.34; it would leave ΔH‡ untouched and move ΔS‡ and ΔG‡. This block's own normalisation prefers the opposite.
  • These are the catalytic increment's parameters. The runs are with_E, so the reference channel omits only the enzyme and the background is already subtracted. The uncatalysed background's own temperature dependence is measured nowhere in the archive.
  • ΔG‡ is far better determined than ΔH‡ or ΔS‡ — ±0.1 against ±1.8 and ±5.9 — because both come from one line and their errors are strongly anti-correlated; at 25 °C, inside the measured range, they cancel. It is propagated with the covariance, not from the two standard errors, which would overstate it several-fold.

5 · Activation energy per rung

Kept because the agreement between the four is the evidence that pooling them is allowed at all.

The 8.5 kJ/mol spread across four fits that ought to agree looked like a composition dependence until the standard errors were computed — each rung is ±2.6–3.0, and every one overlaps the pooled band. The pooled value is refitted over all 24 points rather than averaged from these four, because the four share the same six runs, days and cells: their errors are correlated and averaging would shrink the error by a factor that is not there.

6 · What the slowdown is — and where it is written up

The fall of section 3a is not a property of this block, so its analysis is not on this page. It is product_fate, because the discrimination that settles it needs the whole 4OMe archive: these 23 curves cannot separate “the rate fell with time” from “the rate fell with product” — inside one curve the product only ever grows with time — and the separation comes from 84 curves in which run length and product vary independently.

In one paragraph: the catalysed 4OMe rate falls in proportion to the product it has made, and the same chemistry with no enzyme falls on a clock instead — which is the reference channel these runs are measured against, so its decay is subtracted out. The rate is linear in the product, not hyperbolic in it: production minus a first-order loss of the measured species, not inhibition of the catalyst. It does not happen to BnOH at product concentrations nearly twice as high. Section 5 above has the only consequence for this document, which is none that these six temperatures can resolve.
