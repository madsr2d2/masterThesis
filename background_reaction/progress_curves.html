Progress curves and their fits

All 27 enzyme-free BnOH cuvettes, each with every rate estimator drawn on it. Nothing here is a kinetic-model fit: the mechanism has never been fitted to these curves. These are the empirical rate measurements the analysis rests on.

What is drawn

quadratic through every point — v0 is its slope at t = 0 (headline) least-squares line over the first 20% (shaded) burst/lag form, B ≤ 0, dashed; shaded fan = v0 profile interval straight line through the whole curve

The quadratic is A = c + v0t + at². It chooses no window, uses every point, and is linear in its parameters, so v0 is always identified. Its curvature t is a/se(a): |t| > 3 means the curve measurably bends.

The burst/lag form is A = c + vsst − B(1 − e−t/τ), with v0 = vss − B/τ. It is fitted with B ≤ 0 — the lag branch is excluded because 0 of 16 of these curves pass the acceleration test — and τ is profiled rather than optimised. The shaded fan shows every initial slope allowed by a τ within the 95% cost band. Where that fan is wide the curve does not determine v0, however well the form fits: 21 of 27 curves are bounded by the 30% half-width criterion, and the rest are marked UNBOUNDED.

Fitting well is not the same as being identified. Exp 3 sample 3 is fitted by the unconstrained burst form to better than its own noise, yet across statistically indistinguishable values of τ its v0 ranges from −1.6e-05 to +5.8e-07 — a factor of 28 and a change of sign. That is why v0 is profiled here and why the quadratic, not the burst form, carries the headline numbers.
